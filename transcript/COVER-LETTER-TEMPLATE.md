# Cover Letter Template: Forbes Riley Pitching Framework

Apply the "nobody cares what you do — they care what you can do for them" principle to cover letters. This template walks through each section using the framework from her masterclass.

---

## Before You Write

Gather these inputs:
1. **Job posting** — copy the exact job description
2. **Company background** — what they actually do, their pain points
3. **Your background** — relevant results, credentials
4. **Direct connection** — anything that proves you're not a cold inquiry

---

## Template Structure

### OPENING (2–3 sentences max)
**Goal:** Start with THEIR problem, not your credentials.

**How:** Rephrase the job posting's core challenge back to them, then claim your one-line value.

```
[Company] needs [specific problem from job posting]. I'm [title/credential] who has solved exactly this.
```

**Example (bad):**
> I am excited to apply for the Senior Marketing Manager role. I have 5 years of experience in digital marketing and am passionate about driving results.

**Example (good):**
> You're building a cross-functional team to launch products faster in a crowded market. I'm a product launch strategist who's shipped 7 major releases, averaging 30% faster than industry benchmarks.

---

### QUALIFY ME FAST (1 sentence)
**Goal:** Kill doubt about whether you're worth their time.

**How:** One concrete credential or result that addresses anything they might question (age, background, lack of traditional path, accent, etc.).

```
In my last [timeframe], I [specific measurable result].
```

**Example:**
> In 18 months, I grew a struggling division from $2M to $8.5M revenue — without adding headcount.

---

### WHAT I'LL DO FOR YOU (2–3 sentences)
**Goal:** Frame outcomes for THEM, not skills you have.

**How:** Never say "I have project management expertise." Say "I deliver projects on time and under budget" or "I unblock teams stuck on handoffs."

**Pattern:**
- "You need X" → "I'll deliver Y outcome"
- One sentence per outcome
- Specific beats impressive

```
You'll get [outcome 1]. You'll also [outcome 2]. And [outcome 3 if relevant].
```

**Example (bad):**
> I have extensive experience with stakeholder management and cross-functional collaboration. I am proficient in Agile methodologies and have strong communication skills.

**Example (good):**
> I'll reduce your time-to-launch by cutting internal handoff friction — teams ship instead of waiting. I'll also unblock your engineers from meetings; they'll run Agile, I'll run the dependencies.

---

### PROOF (1–2 sentences)
**Goal:** Make one or two real results real and credible.

**How:** One concrete number outweighs ten adjectives.

**Pattern:**
```
At [company], I [action] and achieved [number + impact].
```

**Example:**
> At my last role, I rebuilt our onboarding flow and cut new-hire ramp-up from 4 months to 6 weeks. That freed up 20% of the team's coaching time in the first quarter alone.

---

### THE CLOSE (1 sentence + call to action)
**Goal:** Be memorable as a human + make it easy to reply "yes."

**How:** 
- One true human detail (not a cliché) that proves something about you
- One clear, specific ask: "I'd like to talk about [specific thing]" or "I'm available [day/time]"

```
[One human line]. I'd like to [specific action]. [How to reach you or when you'll follow up].
```

**Example:**
> I automate the boring stuff so teams can focus on what matters — it's why I started my last project with a workflow audit, not a feature list. I'd like to talk about how I'd approach your launch backlog. I'm free Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon.

---

## Language Rules (Enforced)

**Ban forever:**
- "actually," "basically," "just," "passionate," "hardworking," "results-driven," "amazing," "team player," "synergy," "literally," "honestly," "truly"

**Ratio check:**
- Count "you/your" appearances vs. "I/me" — you/your should win by 2:1 or better

**Specificity test:**
- If you see a vague claim ("changed the culture," "improved efficiency," "drove growth"), rewrite with a number or story

**Jargon audit:**
- Read every term; would this hiring manager use it in conversation? If not, cut it.

---

## Length & Format

- **Maximum 250 words, ideally under 200** — one tight paragraph or two short ones, max
- **Single-spaced, readable font** (Calibri 11 or similar)
- **Addressed to a real name if possible** — "Dear [Hiring Manager Name]" beats "To Whom It May Concern"
- **No generic virtual backgrounds** — write like you're talking to one person on the other side

---

## The Pressure Test

Before you send, answer these:

1. **Does it open with THEIR problem, not my credentials?** (Yes = next; No = rewrite the opening)
2. **Does every sentence either build interest or build trust?** (Count: If >10% are fluff, cut them)
3. **Can I justify every number I included?** (If a stat can't be backed up, remove it)
4. **Would a busy hiring manager stop scrolling and think "this person gets us"?** (If no, the problem framing isn't sharp enough)
5. **Is my call to action so easy they could reply in 10 seconds?** (If it's vague or hard to schedule, tighten it)

## Example: Full Letter (Under 200 words)

> Dear [Name],
>
> You're scaling customer success but your team is drowning in manual churn-prediction work — burning hours on spreadsheets instead of owning retention. I'm a data ops leader who built the automated churn models at [Previous Company], cutting manual work from 20 hours/week to 2, and freed the team to focus on at-risk accounts.
>
> In 14 months, we went from 78% to 91% retention. I didn't do it with a new tool; I automated their existing processes and trained two junior analysts to own the hand-off.
>
> I'd like to talk about how I'd approach your current stack and what we could automate in the first 30 days. I'm available to chat Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon — let me know what works.
>
> [Your Name]
> [Phone]
> [Email]

---

## Tips from the Masterclass

- **Less words, more impact** — every sentence earns its place
- **Say just enough to earn "tell me more"** — your job is to get the interview, not seal the deal in the letter
- **Never read the pitch; embody it** — rewrite until it sounds like you, not a template
- **The Question Flip can work here too** — "Most of your [role] candidates have [experience X]. My path was different because [your reason], and here's what it taught me about [their problem]."
- **Tell a story, don't list specs** — one concrete example beats three bullet points
